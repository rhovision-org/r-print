# The atomisation problem

> **Status:** Orientation. This chapter states the problem; it is not a product claim.

Independent media is atomised. The cost of organising is high, so writers work as
contractors, each brand collapses into a personal brand, and a publication is often a
newsletter with one name on it. Subscription tools solved distribution for individuals but
supply no business logic for a group of people to act as an organisation. There is no
roster, no editorial authority, no commissioned-work escrow, no provenance record, no
corrections ledger, and no reader-revenue split.

The subscription model rewards cadence. A writer who must publish every week to keep churn
low is pushed toward sausage-meat content: quick reactions, listicles, and takes that fit
between two other jobs. Deep research loses, even when readers say they want it, because the
work is a sunk cost with no guaranteed buyer. An investigation can take months. The writer
needs rent and food during those months, and the publication needs a credible promise that
the work will be paid for if it is delivered.

That is an organisational problem, not a writing problem. It has four missing pieces:

1. **A durable organisation.** A masthead that can hold a charter, a roster and a role
   structure independent of any one person's account.
2. **Commissioned work with escrow.** Money committed before the work exists, released on
   milestones, refunded if the work does not arrive.
3. **Provenance that survives the platform.** A content hash, byline, review record and
   append-only correction trail that a reader can inspect.
4. **A path to scale.** Escrow alone becomes dead money: capital sits idle while the
   publication grows. A publication needs an insurance surface, not a bigger purse.

r-print supplies those pieces as small primitives. It does not host content, run an indexer
or replace a writer's relationship with readers. It gives a newsroom the on-chain business
logic that a newsletter tool never had.

The rest of this book explains the model, the contracts, the optional sibling surfaces, and
the honest limits.
