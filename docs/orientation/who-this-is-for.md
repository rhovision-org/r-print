# Who this is for

> **Status:** Orientation.

r-print is for people who want a publication to be an institution rather than a personal
brand. That includes:

- **A small newsroom** of two to ten people who want a shared masthead, a roster, a treasury
  reference and a way to commission work without making every payment a personal favour.
- **A magazine** that publishes in issues and needs an ordered, editor-signed manifest of
  what was in each issue.
- **An investigation desk** that funds long work in stages: a research stipend, a first
  draft, a final delivery, with expenses and a dispute path.
- **A patron or funder** who wants money held against milestones rather than handed over on
  trust, and a defined refund if the work does not arrive.
- **A fork maintainer** who wants the r-* house conventions: Rholang cells, a Python devnet
  harness, a Rust client stack, an mdBook and a Lean 4 formal model with an axiom audit.

It is not for:

- a platform that wants to host content and run an indexer; r-print deliberately does not;
- a writer who wants a one-click newsletter; the CLI and SDK are reference tools, not a
  hosted product;
- a regulated financial product; r-print is devnet-only and unaudited;
- anyone who needs anonymity guarantees; hashes and addresses are public.

The core user is the editor who has to say "we can pay for this, and here is the rule for
what happens if it does not arrive". r-print makes that sentence executable.
