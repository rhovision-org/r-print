# The north star

> **Status:** Orientation. The north star is a design direction, not a shipped feature.

The north star is a newsroom that can outlive its founder. A publication should be able to
hire a writer, commission an investigation, publish the result, correct the record and pay
its people without any of those relationships depending on one platform account. The chain
is the memory: who was on the masthead, what was commissioned, what was delivered, what was
published, what was corrected, and who was paid.

Three properties follow.

**The organisation is first-class.** A publication is not a user profile with a logo. It has
a charter, a roster, roles, a treasury reference, an arbitration rule and a way to continue
when a founder leaves. A solo writer can start with one key, but the model is built so the
publication can grow into a real organisation.

**Work is commissioned, not churned.** A commission holds money against milestones before
the article exists. A stipend can pay for the research phase; a final milestone pays on
delivery. The editor releases against submitted evidence; the funder can cancel or expire
and get the remainder back; a dispute has a defined path.

**Provenance is a public good.** An article record commits to a content hash, bylines,
reviews and corrections. A publication can optionally link the article to an r-gen Claim,
KAsset and Provenance record, which adds a validated knowledge-asset chain without forcing
every writer through it.

The north star is not "put journalism on a blockchain". It is "give a small publication the
business logic of an institution, with the exit door open". Escrow is the starting rail;
r-sure cover is the scale rail; r-gen knowledge assets are the credibility rail. The same
publication can use all three without changing its masthead or its reader relationship.
